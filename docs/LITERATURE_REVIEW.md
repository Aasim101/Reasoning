# Literature Review: LLM Reasoning as of July 2026

**Compiled:** 29 July 2026.
**Verification policy:** every arXiv ID below was either fetched or returned by a live search during compilation. Where an author list was not observed, the entry says *(authors unverified)* rather than guessing. Canonical pre-2024 works are cited from established knowledge and flagged *(canonical)*; their IDs should be spot-checked once before submission. **Nothing here is invented.**

A note on the character of the field. Between 2024 and 2026 LLM reasoning went from a prompting literature to a saturated empirical science with its own theory, its own replication crises, and its own critiques of its critiques. Any review written from a 2024-era model prior will be badly wrong: at least eight distinct 2025–2026 results below reverse, formalise, or pre-empt claims that were open questions eighteen months ago. The review is organised into nine themes, each closing with an explicit statement of what remains unresolved.

---

## 1. Chain-of-thought and its structural variants

**The founding results.** Chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2201.11903, *canonical*) showed that eliciting intermediate steps unlocks multi-step arithmetic and symbolic reasoning in sufficiently large models. Kojima et al. (NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2205.11916, *canonical*) reduced this to a single instruction. Self-consistency (Wang et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv:2203.11171, *canonical*) added the single most consequential idea: sample several chains, take the plurality answer. Its reported +17.9 points on GSM8K with PaLM-540B set the template for everything that followed.

**Structural elaborations.** Least-to-most decomposition (Zhou et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv:2205.10625) generalises to harder compositions than the exemplars. Tree of Thoughts (Yao et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2305.10601) and Graph of Thoughts (Besta et al., AAAI 2024, arXiv:2308.09687) search over partial thoughts with lookahead and backtracking. Program of Thoughts (Chen et al., TMLR 2023, arXiv:2211.12588) and PAL (Gao et al., ICML 2023, arXiv:2211.10435) offload the computation to a Python interpreter, separating planning from arithmetic execution.

**The corrective literature.** Sprague et al. (ICLR 2025, arXiv:2409.12183) meta-analyse across task types and find CoT's benefit is heavily concentrated on mathematical and symbolic reasoning, with little or negative effect elsewhere — a finding that reframes CoT as a domain-specific tool rather than a general reasoning switch. The structural variants have fared less well under matched-budget scrutiny: the 2026 systematic study *The Art of Scaling Test-Time Compute* (arXiv:2512.02008, *authors unverified*) finds across eight open models from 7 B to 235 B and 30 B generated tokens that **no single test-time-scaling strategy dominates**, and that beam search in particular is consistently suboptimal for complex reasoning. A 2026 study of code-mediated reasoning (arXiv:2605.26414, *authors unverified*) finds that neither single-shot PAL-style execution nor iterative step-by-step code improves robustness over plain CoT on GSM-Symbolic, and in fact plain CoT is the most robust of the three.

**What remains unresolved.** (i) There is still no predictive account of *which items* benefit from which structure; every such account is stated in terms of a per-item difficulty whose stability is itself untested. (ii) Whether the residual advantage of tree/graph search over matched-budget sampling is real at small scale, or an artefact of unmatched compute, is not settled. (iii) The interaction between CoT's domain-specificity and the answer-verifiability of the domain is confounded in nearly every study.

## 2. Test-time compute scaling: methods, laws, and ceilings

**Verifiers and process supervision.** Cobbe et al. (arXiv:2110.14168, *canonical*) introduced GSM8K together with the sample-and-rerank recipe. The process-versus-outcome question was posed by Uesato et al. (arXiv:2211.14275) and settled empirically at scale by Lightman et al.'s *Let's Verify Step by Step* (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2305.20050), which showed process supervision beats outcome supervision on MATH and released PRM800K along with the 500-problem MATH subset now universally used. Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., ACL 2024, arXiv:2312.08935) removed the human annotation requirement via automatic per-step labels.

**Scaling laws.** Snell et al. (ICLR 2025, arXiv:2408.03314) is the reference point: the compute-optimal strategy depends on question difficulty (easy questions favour sequential revision, hard ones favour parallel exploration), and under the right allocation test-time compute can beat a 14× larger model — *except* on the hardest questions, where they observe "very little benefit from scaling up test-time compute" and additional pretraining is more effective. Wu et al. (arXiv:2408.00724) develop compute-optimal inference laws. Brown et al.'s *Large Language Monkeys* (arXiv:2407.21787) is the crucial empirical anchor: coverage (pass@n) climbs log-linearly over four orders of magnitude of n, while **majority voting and reward models plateau beyond several hundred samples**. Muennighoff et al.'s s1 (arXiv:2501.19393) showed that a trivially simple budget-forcing intervention plus 1 000 curated examples reproduces much of the benefit.

**The 2025–2026 theory of the plateau — the most important recent development.** Brown et al. reported the plateau without a mechanism. Three independent 2025–2026 lines supply one, all arriving at the same object: the *mode* of the answer distribution.

- Bay & Yearick, *When More Sampling Hurts: The Modal Ceiling and Correlation Ceiling of Test-Time Scaling* (arXiv:2606.28661, June 2026). Two propositions. **Correlation ceiling:** because attempts on one problem are exchangeable and positively correlated, the effective sample count saturates at `n_eff ≤ 1/ρ`; on Brown et al.'s released logs `ρ_b ≈ 0.4–0.6`, so 10⁴ samples per problem carry the benchmark-mean information of roughly **two** independent draws. **Modal ceiling:** plurality voting converges almost surely to the mode, so selection accuracy saturates at the modal-hit rate `π_mode` and *anti-scales* on items whose mode is wrong. The wedge between rising coverage and stalled selection is the **identifiability gap**. Measured: GSM8K / Llama-3-8B-Instruct, coverage 1.00 vs. self-consistency 0.87; on a MATH-500 log with 256 samples per problem, coverage 0.88 vs. a self-consistency plateau of 0.45 reached by n ≈ 64, with a median of only ~13 distinct answers across 256 samples. *Limitation:* it is a re-analysis note over existing logs — no new generation, one model family, and no self-consistency numbers on MATH because their numeric extractor cannot parse boxed answers.
- *Optimal Self-Consistency for Efficient Reasoning* (arXiv:2511.12309, *authors unverified*) derives power-law scaling for self-consistency from mode-estimation and voting theory, and introduces the hyperparameter-free Blend-ASC dynamic allocator (6.8× fewer samples than vanilla SC).
- *Certified Self-Consistency* (arXiv:2510.17472, *authors unverified*) gives finite-sample and anytime-valid certificates that the plurality equals the true mode, with a Martingale Majority Certificate whose stopping time scales as `≈ 2(p_c + p_j)/(p_c − p_j)² · log(1/ε)` in the mode margin, and shows that test-time RL works by exponentially tilting the answer distribution toward its mode.
- *Sample Complexity and Representation Ability of Test-time Scaling Paradigms* (arXiv:2506.05295, *authors unverified*) separates self-consistency's `Θ(1/Δ²)` from best-of-n's `Θ(1/Δ)` in the top-two probability gap.
- *Best-of-Majority* (arXiv:2510.03199, *authors unverified*) proves neither plurality nor best-of-n scales desirably in the pass@k setting and proposes a frequency-then-reward hybrid.

**Where more compute hurts.** Gema et al., *Inverse Scaling in Test-Time Compute* (TMLR 2025, arXiv:2507.14417) construct tasks where longer reasoning **reduces** accuracy, with five distinct failure modes: Claude models get distracted by irrelevant information, o-series models overfit familiar framings, models drift from reasonable priors to spurious correlations, all models lose focus on constraint-tracking deduction, and extended reasoning can amplify concerning behaviours. Relatedly, arXiv:2509.06861 (*authors unverified*) finds test-time scaling ineffective for knowledge-intensive tasks, with longer reasoning often *increasing* hallucination via confirmation bias.

**Surveys.** Zhang et al. (arXiv:2503.24235) organise the field along what/how/where/how-well; arXiv:2501.02497 traces a System-1→System-2 narrative; arXiv:2504.02181 organises by what is scaled; arXiv:2511.14772 organises by subproblem topology.

**What remains unresolved.** (i) Every ceiling result conditions on a per-item latent success rate or answer distribution, and **nobody has tested whether that object is stable under a change of elicitation setting** — this is the gap this project targets. (ii) Bay & Yearick close by suggesting unusable budget "is better spent correlating answers less and choosing among them better" but do not measure how much decorrelation is available. (iii) The identifiability gap's true size is contested, because coverage itself is inflated (see §9). (iv) Nothing reconciles the "no strategy dominates" finding of arXiv:2512.02008 with the per-difficulty prescriptions of Snell et al.

## 3. Long chain-of-thought, RL for reasoning, and the elicit-versus-teach debate

**The models.** DeepSeek-R1 (arXiv:2501.12948) demonstrated that large-scale RL with verifiable rewards, using GRPO from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., arXiv:2402.03300), produces long self-reflective chains and o1-class math performance, and that these behaviours distil into small dense models. Data-efficient distillation followed quickly: s1/s1K (arXiv:2501.19393) and LIMO (arXiv:2502.03387) both argue that a thousand or so well-chosen long chains suffice.

**The debate that defines 2025–2026.** Yue et al. (arXiv:2504.13837) argue via pass@k that RLVR does **not** expand the reasoning boundary beyond the base model: RL-trained models beat base models at small k but are *matched or beaten* at large k, implying RL sharpens a distribution the base model already had. This is the single most-cited contrarian claim in the area, and it has a precise structural analogue in the modal-ceiling literature: both say that the *ordering* of candidate answers, not their presence, is what limits performance.

Two formalisations support the sharpening reading. Huang, Block, Foster, Rohatgi, Zhang, Simchowitz, Ash & Krishnamurthy, *Self-Improvement in Language Models: The Sharpening Mechanism* (ICLR 2025, arXiv:2412.01951) frames self-improvement as using the model as its own verifier to concentrate mass on high-quality sequences, and establishes when SFT-based and RLHF-based sharpening are minimax optimal. *Self-Consistency via Marginal Sharpening* (arXiv:2605.28142, *authors unverified*) recasts self-consistency itself as sharpening the *answer marginal* rather than the joint over traces, recovering plurality voting as the infinite-sharpening limit.

**The cost of sharpening.** *Weight Ensembling Improves Reasoning in Language Models* (arXiv:2504.10478, *authors unverified*) documents diversity collapse: pass@1 reliably improves during SFT while pass@k rapidly deteriorates, because generations converge on a single trace. They derive a **bias–variance decomposition of pass@k** and show WiSE-FT weight interpolation reduces both simultaneously, whereas temperature scaling trades one against the other; they also report a 10–20-point gap between oracle answer-marginal pass@k and what token-level diverse decoding (temperature, top-k, nucleus, min-p) achieves. *Verbalized Sampling* (arXiv:2510.01171, *authors unverified*) attributes mode collapse partly to a typicality bias in preference data and recovers diversity by prompting for an explicit distribution of responses.

**What remains unresolved.** (i) Whether RL genuinely fails to expand capability, or merely fails to do so at the compute scales tested, remains contested; the pass@k methodology is itself under attack (§9). (ii) The sharpening framing implies the candidate *ordering* does all the work, but **the stability of that ordering — under scale, under post-training, and under elicitation — has not been measured.** (iii) The oracle-versus-achievable diversity gap reported in arXiv:2504.10478 has no known test-time-only remedy.

## 4. Faithfulness, premature commitment, and the mechanistic story

**The classical results.** Turpin et al. (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2305.04388) showed models silently follow biasing features in the context while producing rationales that never mention them. Lanham et al. (arXiv:2307.13702) introduced the probe suite that everything since reuses: truncate the chain and force an answer ("early answering"), inject mistakes, paraphrase, add filler. Lyu et al. (arXiv:2301.13379) sidestep the problem with chains that are faithful by construction. A latent-reasoning line asks whether the tokens are needed at all: Coconut (arXiv:2412.06769) reasons in continuous space, and pause/filler-token work (Goyal et al. ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.02226; Pfau et al. COLM 2024, arXiv:2404.15758) shows that meaningless tokens can buy computation, which severs the link between chain content and chain function.

**The 2026 commitment literature.** Two papers sharpen this considerably. *Understanding and Mitigating Premature Confidence for Better LLM Reasoning* (arXiv:2605.24396, *authors unverified*) probes at K evenly spaced truncation points, builds a confidence trajectory, and defines a premature-confidence score (low Spearman correlation between confidence and checkpoint index). It shows premature confidence predicts logical flaws in the chain and mitigates it with progressive confidence shaping, a GRPO advantage penalty using a monotone weight vector. *Decoding Answers Before Chain-of-Thought* (arXiv:2603.01437, *authors unverified*) trains linear probes on residual-stream activations at the last token *before* the CoT and predicts the final answer at >0.9 AUC on most tasks; steering along the probe direction flips the answer in over 50 % of cases, far exceeding orthogonal baselines, establishing causal pre-commitment. When steering induces errors they identify two modes: non-entailment and confabulation.

**The converse decoupling.** *Robust Answers, Fragile Logic* (arXiv:2505.17406, *authors unverified*) introduces MATCHA, an answer-conditioned probing framework, and shows imperceptible input perturbations collapse the rationale while the answer stays correct — "right for the wrong reasons" — most severely on multi-step and commonsense tasks.

**Monitorability.** CoT monitorability became a first-class safety topic in 2025, with a widely signed position paper arguing it is a real but fragile opportunity, work on monitoring frontier reasoning models for reward hacking, and Anthropic's finding that reasoning models frequently use hints without verbalising them. *These specific 2025 citations were not re-verified in this session and are marked UNVERIFIED; the code agent should confirm arXiv:2507.11473 (monitorability position paper), arXiv:2505.05410 (reasoning models don't always say what they think) and arXiv:2503.11926 (monitoring reward hacking) before they enter the bibliography.*

**What remains unresolved.** (i) These results establish that the model often has an answer early, but **none connects that to the selection bottleneck at the heart of test-time scaling** — if the answer is fixed early, then what a conditioning change perturbs is the early commitment, which is exactly the operation resampling cannot perform. (ii) Faithfulness is measured as a model-level average; its dependence on per-item difficulty is largely unmeasured. (iii) Whether monitorability degrades as test-time compute increases is open.

## 5. Overthinking, underthinking, and efficient reasoning

Reasoning models overspend catastrophically on trivial items (*Do NOT Think That Much for 2+3=?*, arXiv:2412.21187) and, conversely, thrash between abandoned strategies on hard ones (*Thoughts Are All Over the Place*, arXiv:2501.18585). The efficiency literature is now large enough to have its own survey (*Stop Overthinking*, arXiv:2503.16419). Control mechanisms include budget forcing (arXiv:2501.19393), length-controlled policy optimisation (L1, arXiv:2503.04697), and vendor thinking-budget parameters and hybrid thinking/non-thinking modes.

On the parallel axis, adaptive allocation is comprehensively solved — which matters, because it was one of this project's candidate contributions and had to be abandoned. Adaptive-Consistency (Aggarwal et al., EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2305.11860) stops when a Dirichlet posterior says the vote is decided; Early-Stopping Self-Consistency (Li et al., 2024, arXiv:2401.10480) stops on unanimous windows; Certaindex/Dynasor (Fu et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2412.20993) generalises to an algorithm-agnostic "answer stabilisation" metric usable across CoT, self-consistency, MCTS and Rebase, and wires it into a serving scheduler for up to 50 % compute savings and 3.3× throughput; Blend-ASC (arXiv:2511.12309) allocates across questions hyperparameter-free; the Martingale Majority Certificate (arXiv:2510.17472) gives an anytime-valid stopping rule; and a 2026 verifier-guided adaptive framework (arXiv:2602.01070, *authors unverified*) shows online verification beats post-hoc reranking but degrades on the hardest problems where the PRM misranks.

**What remains unresolved.** (i) Every one of these estimates a per-item quantity in one setting and consumes it in another; **none validates that the estimate transfers.** (ii) PRM quality is the acknowledged bottleneck for the verifier-guided variants, and it degrades precisely where headroom is largest. (iii) Overthinking and underthinking are described as separate pathologies with no unified account of when a model does which.

## 6. Robustness, perturbation, and reasoning versus memorisation

Mirzadeh et al.'s GSM-Symbolic (ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.05229) is the anchor: templated GSM8K variants reveal high variance across instantiations of the same template, degradation as clause count rises (M1 → P1 → P2), and — the headline — up to 65 % drops on **GSM-NoOp**, where a seemingly relevant but operationally irrelevant clause is added. They argue the failure is not repairable by few-shot examples of the same phenomenon, and conclude models pattern-match rather than reason formally.

Supporting evidence for fragility comes from contamination audits (GSM1k, arXiv:2405.00332), counterfactual task variants (*Reasoning or Reciting?*, NAACL 2024, arXiv:2307.02477), premise-order effects (arXiv:2402.08939), the reversal curse (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2309.12288), and compositionality analysis (*Faith and Fate*, NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2305.18654).

**The rebuttal literature is active and matters.** An ICLR 2025 blogpost re-runs the GSM-Symbolic statistics and confirms the NoOp drop is highly significant, while explicitly calling for the ablation of telling the model that "not all information might be relevant". A March 2026 replication (non-archival; **UNVERIFIED**, currently a personal write-up plus a GitHub/HF release) reports that when NoOp distractors are audited by an independent judge for genuine irrelevance, the drop essentially vanishes on 2026 frontier models — suggesting the original effect substantially reflects models reasonably treating ambiguous clauses as informative. The Tower-of-Hanoi "Illusion of Thinking" critique and its rebuttals follow the same pattern and are **not re-verified here**; the code agent should confirm before citing. Separately, arXiv:2605.26414 finds code execution does not repair GSM-Symbolic robustness.

**What remains unresolved.** (i) Whether NoOp-style failures are a reasoning deficit or a pragmatics/relevance prior is genuinely open, and the field's headline number may be an artefact. (ii) All of these perturb the **problem**; almost nobody perturbs only the **elicitation** while holding the problem fixed, which is the cleaner isolation. (iii) There is no accepted protocol for validating that a "semantics-preserving" perturbation actually preserves semantics.

## 7. Self-correction, critics, and debate

Huang et al. (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.01798) established the negative result: without external feedback, intrinsic self-correction does not reliably improve reasoning and often degrades it, and earlier positive results leaned on oracle stopping signals. Kamoi et al.'s TACL 2024 survey (arXiv:2406.01297) systematises when self-correction can work, concluding it requires information the generator did not already use. Self-Refine (arXiv:2303.17651) and multi-agent debate (ICML 2024, arXiv:2305.14325) remain the reference positive-claim methods, both subsequently criticised for underperforming plain self-consistency at matched compute.

**What remains unresolved.** (i) Whether long-CoT RLVR models genuinely self-verify, or merely emit self-verification-shaped text, is unsettled; the "aha moment" narrative has not survived close scrutiny. (ii) Error *detection* within a long chain remains the bottleneck, and PRMs degrade exactly on hard items. (iii) Almost all self-correction evaluation lacks the matched-token discipline the parallel-sampling literature now enforces.

## 8. Uncertainty, calibration, and confidence in reasoning chains

Model self-knowledge (Kadavath et al., arXiv:2207.05221), semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv:2302.09664; Farquhar et al., *Nature* 2024), and verbalised confidence (Lin et al., TMLR 2022, arXiv:2205.14334; Tian et al., EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2305.14975) supply the signal vocabulary. In reasoning specifically, vote share is the default confidence, and a dense 2024–2026 cluster tries to beat it: universal self-consistency (arXiv:2311.17311), program-verified voting (PROVE, arXiv:2410.12608), semantic weighting of reasoning paths (arXiv:2410.07839), confidence-informed self-consistency and its cluster-based efficient variant VecCISC (ACL Findings 2026, arXiv:2605.08070), learned rerankers over answer-level features (RISC, ACL SRW 2026, arXiv:2606.05054), embedding-space radial consensus (arXiv:2604.12196, *title and authors unverified*), and best-of-majority hybrids (arXiv:2510.03199).

The framing that unifies these: the correct answer is frequently *present but not modal*, so the problem is **selection**, not generation. RISC states this explicitly as the gap between majority-vote accuracy and oracle selection.

**What remains unresolved.** (i) All of this searches for a better selector over a **fixed** sample pool; the prior question of what determines the pool's mode is barely asked. (ii) Whether long-CoT post-training improves or degrades calibration relative to the base model is not settled. (iii) No confidence signal has been shown to work well on precisely the confidently-wrong items where it would matter most.

## 9. Benchmarks and evaluation methodology: what a 2026 reviewer expects

**Datasets.** GSM8K (`openai/gsm8k`, 1 319 test items) and MATH-500 (`HuggingFaceH4/MATH-500`, **500 rows, verified by fetch**) are the workhorses; MATH-500 is the PRM800K subset from Lightman et al. AIME/AMC sets, GPQA-Diamond, BBH, MuSR, FOLIO, ZebraLogic, LiveBench and ARC-AGI fill out the range. `apple/GSM-Symbolic` supplies templated perturbations. *Exact HF IDs and row counts for everything except MATH-500 and GSM8K should be confirmed at load time; several community mirrors have diverged and the original Hendrycks MATH repo has had availability issues.*

**Statistical hygiene is now a gating criterion, not a nicety.** Miller (arXiv:2411.00640) made interval reporting a norm. Hochlehnert et al. (arXiv:2504.07086) showed that a large share of recently reported progress on small reasoning benchmarks is seed variance and that many results do not reproduce — which is why AIME-scale evaluations moved to avg@k over many seeds. *Don't Pass@k* (arXiv:2510.04265, *authors unverified*) proposes Bayesian posterior reporting with credible intervals in place of pass@k and avg@N, noting that CLT-based intervals are invalid in the small-sample regime typical of these evals.

**Two 2025 results that specifically undercut coverage-based claims.** *Beyond Pass@k* (arXiv:2510.08325, *authors unverified*) proves pass@k is a Beta(1,k)-weighted average of a reliability-controlled coverage `Cover@τ`, hence structurally biased toward low-reliability lucky hits, and shows this reorders the ranking of RLVR methods. *Examining False Positives under Inference Scaling for Mathematical Reasoning* (EMNLP 2025 main, arXiv:2502.06217, *authors unverified*) shows that final-answer matching admits solutions with flawed deductions at a material rate, that sampling-based inference scaling does **not** alleviate this, and that pass@N is the metric most susceptible — implying a substantially lower true scaling ceiling than automatic evaluation reports.

**Psychometrics.** A 2025–2026 line fits item response theory to LLM response matrices: IrtNet (arXiv:2510.00844), ATLAS adaptive testing (arXiv:2511.04689, up to 90 % item reduction), PSN-IRT for benchmark diagnosis (AAAI), and IRT-Router for routing (ACL 2025). Commentary notes that Rasch and 2PL models fit AI benchmark data surprisingly well, and that per-problem exponential scaling aggregating into aggregate power laws is an IRT statement in disguise.

**Ensemble limits.** Kim et al., *Correlated Errors in Large Language Models* (ICML 2025, PMLR v267) evaluate 350+ models and find substantial error correlation — 60 % agreement conditional on both being wrong on one leaderboard — that *increases* with capability even across architectures and providers. A 2026 follow-up (arXiv:2606.27288, *authors unverified*) formalises a co-failure ceiling bounding every selection policy including debate and self-consistency, and reports that the effective number of independent perspectives in model panels is often only ~2–2.5.

**What remains unresolved.** (i) The community has not converged on a replacement for pass@k despite three credible proposals. (ii) The IRT line assumes unidimensional item difficulty and validates it largely on non-reasoning benchmarks; **the assumption has not been stress-tested for reasoning items with elicitation as a crossed factor** — the gap this project targets. (iii) There is no standard for reporting answer-extraction failure rates, even though extraction quality demonstrably determines measured selection accuracy.

## Synthesis: the shape of the field in mid-2026

Three convergent findings define the moment. **First**, the bottleneck has moved from generation to selection: coverage says the right answer is usually in the pool, while plurality voting is capped at the modal-hit rate and reward models plateau. **Second**, most of what looks like capability gain — from RLVR, from distillation, from self-improvement — is formally *sharpening* an ordering the base model already had, which is why pass@k analyses keep finding the boundary unmoved. **Third**, the field's measurements are less solid than they look: coverage is inflated by lucky hits and false positives, seed variance explains a chunk of reported progress, and errors are correlated far more than ensemble methods assume.

Every one of these three results is stated in terms of a per-item latent quantity — a difficulty, a success rate, an answer distribution — that is estimated once and treated as a property of the item. The stability of that quantity is the field's most consequential untested assumption, and it is what this project measures.
